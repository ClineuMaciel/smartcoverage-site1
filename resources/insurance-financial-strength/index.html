Four things to compare before choosing a policy

1. Premium

Compare the total cost over the same policy period. Ask about installment fees and discount conditions. A lower price alone does not establish that a policy is unsuitable or that an insurer is financially weak.

2. Coverage limits

Review the amounts available for covered property damage and liability claims. Ask the quoting agent how the proposed limits fit the home, vehicle and financial responsibilities you describe.

3. Deductibles and exclusions

Understand your share of a covered loss and the events or property the policy excludes. Compare similar coverage when reviewing prices; different terms can produce different premiums.

4. Insurer financial strength

Review current independent financial-strength ratings for the exact company issuing the policy. Washington’s insurance regulator recommends checking an insurer’s financial rating before buying. [1]

Financial ratings and customer reviews answer different questions

A financial-strength rating concerns the insurer’s financial capacity. Consumer reviews describe individual experiences and are not a substitute for that assessment. New Jersey’s regulator recommends considering financial stability, policyholder service and claims handling, and notes that rating companies use different methods. [2]

Check the rating agency, rating date and meaning of its scale. Ask for the exact underwriting company’s legal name rather than relying only on a familiar brand name. A rating is not a guarantee that a particular claim will be paid or that the company will meet every future obligation. [3]

Bring these questions to your quoting agent

  • Which company will issue my policy?
  • Where can I verify its authorization to offer this coverage in my state?
  • What is its current financial-strength rating, and which agency issued it?
  • What property and liability limits are included?
  • Which deductibles, exclusions and settlement conditions should I understand?
  • How does this quote compare with my current policy on equivalent terms?

Having a policy and understanding your protection are two different things. Reviewing both cost and coverage can help you make a more informed choice before a major accident or property loss.
